EMS Exposure Forecasting App

A lightweight dashboard that turns historical work-related exposure events into interpretable weekly forecasts, accuracy checks, and manager-ready planning advice.

Outcome
Weekly exposure counts
Stack
SQL + Python + Streamlit
Goal
Proactive safety planning
Problem and motivation

EMS leaders often see risk only after a bad week

Why it matters

EMS clinicians work in unpredictable scenes, under time pressure, with lifting strain, transport hazards, and blood or body-fluid exposure risk.

Gap

Most systems still monitor injuries and exposures retrospectively, which limits proactive staffing, PPE messaging, and training decisions.

Project question

Can simple, transparent forecasting methods turn routine exposure records into short-term signals that managers can actually use?

Research focus
1. Surface recent patterns
Build clear visual summaries of exposure events.
2. Test temporal structure
Check whether weekly exposure counts show trend or seasonality.
3. Communicate uncertainty
Show forecast error with MAE, RMSE, and simple planning language.
Data pipeline

Pragmatic design: one clean outcome, one usable weekly series

🗂️
Source data

The final scope used a cleaned 2024 cohort of work-related exposure events rather than multi-year demand and injury modeling.

⚙️
Engineering flow

SQL Server handled heavy filtering and joins, then exported a compact CSV that Python ingested into the app.

📈
Forecast-ready series

The app parsed timestamps, built daily counts, then resampled to Monday-based weekly exposure totals for modeling.

Pipeline summary
Raw exposure records
→
SQL cleaning & cohort build
→
CSV → daily counts → weekly series
Methods

Simple models first, then explicit backtesting

Forecasting menu

Naive, moving average, exponential smoothing, Holt, Holt-Winters, SARIMA, and one clearly labeled experimental random forest option.

Evaluation

A hold-out backtest on recent weeks reports MAE, RMSE, MAPE, and MAE as a share of average weekly exposure volume.

Design principle

The project prioritizes interpretability over black-box performance so non-technical managers can judge reliability before acting.

Impact, limits, next steps

From passive dashboard to decision support

Main contribution

The project shows that routine EMS exposure records can support a transparent weekly forecasting workflow that managers can understand and use.

Current limits

The final prototype is constrained by one year of data, a single exposure outcome, and incomplete hospital outcome fields.

Next phase

Extend to more years, validate model performance across settings, and strengthen links between forecasts, staffing, and preventive actions.
